# Educational data

Datasets here are small, synthetic, and created solely for education. Chapter 1's
`harbor_incident_telemetry.csv` describes fictional operational measurements; it
contains no member or transaction records. Do not add real member, transaction,
credential, vendor, or proprietary banking data.

Chapters 2 and 3 use `harbor_request_outcomes.csv`, which contains 30
deterministic, fictional vendor-request observations and binary teaching labels.
It makes feature/target separation and a small train/test pipeline executable; it
is not statistically realistic and does not represent a real banking distribution.

Chapter 4 uses `harbor_normal_telemetry.csv`, a committed baseline of 200
deterministically generated ordinary-operation observations, and
`harbor_anomaly_scenarios.csv`, six named evaluation observations kept out of
training. The scenarios cover normal behavior, vendor slowdown, queue pressure,
broad pressure, and a subtle multivariate combination. The fixtures are stable for
the executable laboratory; they are not estimates of real production distributions
or Harbor's true incident rate.

Chapter 5 uses `harbor_incident_classes.csv`, a deterministic, balanced fixture of
300 labeled observations (60 per teaching class). Its overlapping synthetic
patterns support a multi-class classification exercise. The balance and values do
not estimate real incident frequency or describe a real production taxonomy.

Chapter 7 uses `harbor_integration_requests.csv`, 600 deterministic request-level
observations generated by `scripts/generate_integration_requests.py` with fixed
seed 707. Its overlapping binary outcomes combine fictional recent vendor metrics,
queue and retry pressure, request size, and modest endpoint interactions with
noise. No vendor is a deterministic label. The rules are educational and do not
represent real integration reliability, members, financial activity, or production
data.

Chapters 8 and 9 use `harbor_member_events.csv`, 3,248 events across 390 fictional sessions,
generated by `scripts/generate_member_events.py` with fixed seed 808. Its minimal
schema contains timestamps, pseudonymous session IDs, event names, web/mobile
channels, and coarse journey names. It contains no direct member identifier,
financial value, credential, or authentication data. Generation patterns support
event grouping and descriptive analysis. Three hundred additional transfer sessions
support Chapter 9's partial-journey classification with noisy, overlapping synthetic
relationships and both outcomes; they do not estimate real member behavior.
